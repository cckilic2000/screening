## Executive Summary

UNDP Moldova has issued RfP26/03219 for two linked energy-sector feasibility exercises: RNLC and PNME. The assignment covers institutional ownership, legal requirements, data readiness, interoperability, options appraisal and costed implementation/legislative roadmaps, with a final PNME study at 12 months. Bidders must provide six mandatory specialist roles and meet corporate experience and financial-capacity requirements; the scoring gives significant attention to Moldova’s state-information framework and practical experience. Proposals are due through Quantum on 25 November 2026 at 10:30 New York time.

## Detailed Assignment Summary

The RNLC pillar must establish whether and how a persistent national consumption-point register can be constituted, including institutional ownership, four-object identity design, unique identifiers, source authority, data quality and interaction with existing government and energy systems. It uses measured samples from Premier Energy Distribution and RED-Nord, compares reuse and architecture options, and delivers a GO/CONDITIONAL GO/NO-GO conclusion with regulatory/procurement preparation. The operating concept in Annex 1 is background, not a predetermined institutional or technical solution.

The PNME pillar assesses the wider energy-data ecosystem and defines justified, costed institutional and technical options for energy imports, production and consumption information. Its outputs address interoperability with RNLC/SINEE, official statistics, governance, privacy, cybersecurity, operational resilience and future institutional adoption. Binding Annexes 2 and 3 prescribe the report structures; detailed software design, development/implementation and drafting the full RNLC normative package are outside this assignment.

The intended decision-makers and users include the Ministry of Energy, ANRE, the e-Governance Agency, STISC, the National Centre for Sustainable Energy, statistical/reference-register institutions and energy operators. Evidence is shared across the pillars to avoid duplicate requests, and the final models, inventories, matrices, scripts and supporting material must be editable and usable by counterpart institutions.

## Key Deliverables

- D1.1: RNLC inception report and consolidated data/information request package for both pillars; T0 + two weeks.
- D1.2: As-Is/institutional/legal baseline, operator maturity/readiness, system and dataset inventory, process diagrams and legal gap register; T0 + seven weeks.
- D1.3: measured live-data readiness/profiling and reconciliation diagnostic, readiness gates, thresholds and operator remediation estimates, with editable scripts/results; T0 + nine weeks.
- D2.1: business analysis, four-object identity model, identifier design/lifecycle, use cases, logical data model and system/source-authority delineation; T0 + 11 weeks.
- D2.2: documented options appraisal, business case, EU alignment, international benchmark and editable costing model; T0 + 12 weeks.
- D3.1: RNLC rapid feasibility report with readiness decision, operating/data-governance model, risk register, indicators and phased roadmap, plus validation workshop; T0 + 13 weeks.
- D3.2: Romanian annotated outlines/drafting instructions for the Concept, Regulation and technical specification; legislative roadmap, procurement strategy and knowledge-transfer session. If NO-GO, provide remediation/readiness/legislative outputs instead; timing as stated in Tender Details.
- D4.1: PNME inception note, methodology and workplan coordinated with Pillar A; T0 + three weeks.
- D4.2: PNME As-Is report, system/dataset inventory, reporting obligations, process diagrams, readiness indicators, official-statistics reconciliation and benchmark; T0 + five months.
- D5.1: PNME needs, prioritised use-case catalogue, traceable requirements and integration specifications; T0 + seven months.
- D5.2: at least three implementation options, shared-foundation option, multi-criteria appraisal, conceptual operating model/business case and editable costing; T0 + nine months.
- D5.3: institutional/legal/data-governance report, legal gap register, source-authority/delineation/RACI matrices and privacy/cybersecurity/resilience annex; T0 + nine months.
- D6.1: phased implementation roadmap covering one to three years of future implementation, institutional adoption/operating-transition plan, indicator catalogue and risk register; T0 + 10.5 months.
- D6.2: consolidated PNME feasibility study and editable annexes, implementation-readiness classification, validation workshop and knowledge-transfer session; T0 + 12 months.
- For every deliverable, provide an acceptance checklist mapping chapters, evidence, validation, assumptions, access/legal limitations, editable annexes, quality assurance and required decisions.

## Capacity Building Components

- Hold institutional/operator interviews and working sessions, validate responsibilities and interaction scenarios, and convene a validation workshop for each pillar.
- Deliver an RNLC handover/knowledge-transfer session and a PNME handover/knowledge-transfer session with presentation/materials.
- Provide editable models, scripts, inventories and matrices so beneficiary institutions can rerun and update the analysis.
- Prepare the PNME adoption/transition plan, including staffing and training needs, process ownership, support/escalation, onboarding, change control and recurrent budget responsibilities.
- Demonstrate the proposed knowledge-transfer/national-capacity approach in the technical methodology; identifying future training needs is distinct from implementing a full future training programme.

## Team Requirements

- All countries are eligible. A legal entity or consortium needs at least three years’ relevant ICT/digital-government/feasibility/data/energy advisory activity; a JV lead needs three years and each partner two.
- At least one completed relevant multi-source data platform/hub/warehouse or sectoral reporting assignment in the preceding seven years, supported by the specified reference evidence.
- Average turnover of at least USD 250,000 over the last three years and average current-assets/current-liabilities ratio at least 1; provide 2023–2025 audited financial evidence. The RFP allows financial-capacity investigation where the ratio is below 1.
- Six mandatory roles: Team Leader/Project Manager; Energy Sector Expert; Data Architecture/Data Governance Expert; IT/E-government Systems Architecture Expert; Financial/Cost-Benefit Analysis Expert; Legal/Government IT Governance Expert. One person may cover at most two roles, with separate evaluation for each.
- Scored profiles cover relevant degrees and first experience levels of eight years for the leader; five for energy; three for data; five for IT architecture; three for financial/CBA; five for legal/government-IT work. Specific assignments, Moldova-government/energy/system experience and English/Romanian abilities are separately scored. The TOR states a missing first-level subcriterion earns zero there rather than automatically rejecting the expert.
- Indicative working days by role: 85, 70, 90, 65, 45 and 50 respectively; bidders may justify changes in effort/allocation. Additional non-key personnel are optional and not scored.
- English proposals; English outputs except the RNLC Romanian executive summary and full Romanian normative-outline package. In-person Chișinău engagement is expected during data collection/consultation.
- A consortium is optional. Submit Form E and signed intent/agreement; the selected proposer must provide the notarised agreement before signature, identify the lead and ensure joint and several liability.

- The contractor and affiliated/related entities may not participate in the resulting RNLC/PNME design, development or implementation procurement.
- No advance payment; stated delay damages are 2.5% per week, capped at 10%, with exclusions for beneficiary/UNDP-attributable delays.

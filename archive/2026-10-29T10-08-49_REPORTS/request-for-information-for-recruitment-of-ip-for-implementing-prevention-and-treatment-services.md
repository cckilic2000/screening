## Executive Summary

UNODC Afghanistan is requesting information from non-profit partners to implement prevention and treatment services under a JICA-funded project. The scope covers three existing women/children’s treatment centres, two new outpatient centres, family prevention and quality-assurance/capacity-building activities across five provinces. Eligible organisations need at least three years of relevant implementation experience, target-province presence or experience, and a recent annual budget above USD 750,000. The planned agreement is up to 12 months, with submissions due by 16:30 Kabul time on 18 November 2026.

## Detailed Assignment Summary

The project aims to improve access to evidence-based drug-demand-reduction services and treatment quality for people using drugs and affected communities, particularly women and children. It supports existing centres in Helmand, Kandahar and Badakhshan and establishes outpatient services in Badghis and Takhar. The annex sets treatment-completion targets of 480 across existing centres and 320 through the two new outpatient centres.

Prevention activities use the Strong Families Programme. Quality-assurance work includes regular service assessments, centre-specific improvement plans, follow-up, clinical governance and a national review workshop. For several training, QA and exposure activities, UNODC or its experts deliver the technical content while the implementing partner provides coordination, travel, venues, catering, allowances and other operational support; the report preserves that division of responsibilities.

## Key Deliverables

- Support three existing women/children’s drug-treatment centres and establish two outpatient centres, with the stated treatment-completion targets.
- Support selection of 24 national Strong Families facilitators from Badghis and Takhar and logistical delivery of a two-day ToT in Kabul.
- Support family-skills prevention delivery: the detailed annex targets 4,000 caregiver-child pairs/families; the main RFI table instead targets 8,000 families.
- Implement/support QA appraisal at eight treatment centres, with eight improvement plans and follow-up reporting; support a national QA review workshop.
- Translate the key treatment-service quality standards into Dari and Pashto; the indicators also refer to three translated training curricula.
- Support TreatNet ToT for 20 Afghan experts and training of 60 treatment staff using TreatNet packages A, B and C.
- Provide logistics for one Iran exposure visit involving 20 service providers/stakeholders and support two synthetic/polydrug treatment trainings per year, 20 participants each.
- Support three drug-demand-reduction training courses in the first year, 25 technical/official participants per course, and additional learning exchanges as specified.
- RFI-stage submission: legal, organisational, governance and financial evidence; technical experience and key-personnel CVs; implementation methodology and workplan; referenced Annex 2 eligibility self-assessment.

## Capacity Building Components

- Two-day Strong Families ToT in Kabul and support for cascade delivery of family prevention activities.
- TreatNet Training of Trainers for 20 professionals and clinical training for 60 service staff.
- Regular QA mentoring, appraisal, improvement planning and national review.
- Exposure visit to Iran and capacity development on synthetic/polydrug treatment and harm reduction.
- Three courses for technical teams and officials; translation into Dari and Pashto.
- For designated activities, the implementing partner provides logistical support while UNODC/technical experts provide training content and facilitation.

## Team Requirements

- Eligible applicants are non-profit CSOs/NGOs/CBOs registered under Afghan law and/or with a legal permit to operate in Afghanistan; they must directly prepare and manage the project.
- At least three years of experience in prevention/treatment, QA and capacity building; major multilateral/bilateral donor experience; presence or relevant past work in a target province; work with people who use drugs is required.
- Most recently completed annual income/budget for 2024 or 2025 must exceed USD 750,000; an account capable of receiving international deposits is required. This is a qualification threshold, not the procurement budget.
- Supply organisational structure, governance arrangements, technical expertise, key-position CVs, recent financial/audit evidence and implementation methodology/workplan.
- The scope identifies doctors, nurses, psychologists, counsellors, social workers and treatment managers as training beneficiaries; it does not provide a mandatory bidder staffing matrix or degree/year minima for individual experts.
- Required individual expert qualifications and consortium rules: COULDN'T BE FOUND.

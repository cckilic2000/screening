## Executive Summary

UNDP Kyrgyzstan is procuring twelve months of support for implementation of the ILO Convention 190 Road map. The scope includes legal reform, institutional guidance, training of trainers, regional capacity building and communication on workplace violence and harassment. A care-economy review will inform gender-responsive social-protection recommendations. Proposals are due through Quantum on 13 November 2026 at 08:00 New York time.

## Detailed Assignment Summary

The provider will work with national partners to review at least ten legal acts against ILO Convention 190, support a working group, consult stakeholders, prepare a legislative amendment package and assist the adoption process. A national validation workshop in Bishkek is to include at least sixty in-person participants, including fourteen from regions, and ten online participants. Institutional guidelines and training materials will support prevention, confidential/anonymous complaint channels and response without retaliation.

The capacity-building programme includes Russian- and Kyrgyz-language general and ToT materials, two two-day ToTs in Bishkek with at least thirty-five participants each, and two-day trainings for at least thirty-five participants each in Bishkek and Osh. A communication strategy and products complement implementation. A gender-responsive care-economy review, using government data and stakeholder evidence, will produce policy recommendations and a validation workshop with forty participants.

## Key Deliverables

- Comparative legislative review of at least ten acts, working-group support and validated recommendations.
- National consultation/validation workshop and documentation; draft law/amendment package and support to the legislative process.
- Russian/Kyrgyz training methodology, general training package and ToT package.
- Two two-day ToTs in Bishkek, plus two-day capacity-building events in Bishkek and Osh, with the specified participant groups and minimum numbers.
- Institutional workplace GBV/harassment prevention and response guidelines, including complaints arrangements.
- Communication strategy, campaign toolkit and visibility/media products.
- Care-economy assessment, gender-responsive recommendations and forty-participant validation workshop.
- Interim reporting at months 1 and 6 and final reporting at month 12, with supporting activity and handover materials.
- Care-economy recommendations in Kyrgyz, Russian and English, validation-meeting interpretation between those languages, and formal handover of all developed documents to the Ministry of Labour, Social Welfare and Migration.
- Public media/communication strategy and locally adapted content package: unified visual identity, videos/audio, infographics, survivor stories, social-media content and employer/trade-union guidance, developed with the designated national partners.

## Capacity Building Components

- Two two-day ToTs, each with at least thirty-five participants, covering government bodies and labour/employer/business/civil-society stakeholders.
- Two-day training in Bishkek and two-day training in Osh, each with at least thirty-five participants from the respective regions.
- Bilingual Russian/Kyrgyz training methodology, curricula/materials and institutional guidance.
- Working-group facilitation, national validation, policy dialogue and transfer of workplace prevention/response tools.
- Guidance builds designated institutional focal points’ capacity to introduce and maintain confidential workplace reporting channels; the system must track complaints, permit anonymous disclosures, protect against retaliation and support aggregate transparency reporting.

## Team Requirements

- Registered organization with at least five years’ institutional experience in legislative analysis related to labour/employment and at least five years in GBV training/capacity building, including workplace violence/harassment prevention or response.
- Legal Expert/Team Leader: university degree in law, public administration, social policy or related field; legislative review/drafting and labour/employment experience; gender/GBV/social-inclusion experience is evaluated.
- GBV Expert/Trainer: relevant degree in gender/social sciences, law, human rights, public policy, sociology or development; GBV/workplace programming and capacity-building experience, including at least one relevant project.
- Social Protection Expert: social-sciences degree; social-protection financing, budgeting/expenditure analysis, government data/national accounts and care-economy assessment experience.
- Media Expert on GBV: relevant communications/journalism/media/PR/marketing/development/social-science degree and experience in GBV/harassment strategies and multimedia products.
- All four profiles require fluent Russian and Kyrgyz; English is an advantage. Joint ventures/consortia/associations may combine experience with the required legal documentation. Proposed experts cannot appear in competing proposals for this RFP.

## Executive Summary

WHO Ukraine invites proposals under RFP 2026/EURO/UKR/0220 for four mass-casualty-management trainings and four full-scale facility simulation exercises. The contract period is 1 November 2026 to 30 April 2027, with proposals due 9 October. The TOR requires WHO-certified instructors, post-training mentoring, response-plan work and formal reports. The final commercial values are left for the solicitation or contract and are not stated as a budget.

## Detailed Assignment Summary

The provider will organize four blended courses using the current WHO MCM package, including online orientation, face-to-face learning and tabletop exercises for multidisciplinary teams. Facility teams will receive mentoring to review or revise their emergency response and mass-casualty plans. The provider will then design and conduct four full-scale simulation exercises, with evaluation, debriefing and corrective-action findings. The RFP includes printing and basic-kit annexes and a financial proposal template; payment percentage fields are blank placeholders rather than published amounts.

## Key Deliverables

- Four blended MCM trainings with tabletop exercises, attendance, pre/post assessments, certificates and training reports.
- Post-training mentoring and consolidated mentoring report.
- Four full-scale facility-based simulation exercises, exercise reports, corrective-action findings and consolidated cross-site analysis.

## Capacity Building Components

- MCM training for multidisciplinary EMS and healthcare teams.
- Post-training mentoring on facility emergency and mass-casualty response plans.
- Simulation design, conduct, evaluation and debriefing to strengthen national training/exercise capacity.

## Team Requirements

Applicant must be a national organization, medical-training institution, healthcare facility or other legal entity authorized to operate in Ukraine; active in emergency medical or directly relevant training, with CPD/CME accreditation or arrangement. At least six instructors per training, including two WHO-certified MCM instructors, are required. A joint-venture/consortium annex is provided; proposals are in English.

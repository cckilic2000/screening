## Executive Summary

National Grid Company of Pakistan Limited is procuring a firm to implement biodiversity action plans financed by the Asian Development Bank. The assignment combines protected-area governance, forest enhancement, bird and turtle monitoring, stakeholder engagement and conservation training over up to five years. The CSRN Profile publishes a USD 669,813 maximum budget, while its Cost Estimate totals USD 669,812. Expressions of interest are due on 27 November 2026.

## Detailed Assignment Summary

The firm will lead day-to-day delivery of conservation measures for transmission-line subprojects S1, S2 and S4, appointing a BAP Team Leader and managing the required experts and support staff. Activities include protected-area management support, approximately 5.4 hectares of forest enhancement, ten three-day bird surveys per year over five years, three annual turtle-survey visits, training for government departments and communities, and awareness work on illegal turtle trade. A turtle-breeding and nest-protection programme is contingent on monitoring findings and the updated critical-habitat assessment. NGC, protected-area managers, relevant government departments and local communities receive the resulting monitoring information, conservation support and training.

## Key Deliverables

- Implementation and reporting for all S1/S2 and S4 biodiversity action items, including annual progress reporting and data needed for quarterly reports.
- Additional annual Biodiversity Management Committee meetings over three years, with meeting records and information dissemination.
- Forest-enhancement site assessments, site-selection evidence, mapped method statements, planting and maintenance arrangements, and quarterly monitoring over three years.
- Five-year bird-monitoring datasets, analyses, reports, survey guidance and scientific publications; ten three-day surveys annually.
- Annual bird-conservation community seminars and village-head meetings, plus the specified IUCN MKKS management-team workshops.
- Turtle-survey protocols, questionnaires, remote survey-team training and monitoring results from three annual field visits.
- Government and community training materials, at least five community-stakeholder training sessions, and an awareness plan on poaching and illegal trade.
- A written summary supporting a potential national turtle-species recovery plan and CITES-related technical advice.
- If activated, turtle-breeding site specifications, breeding and release protocols, staff training, land arrangements and implementation reporting.
- Specified equipment purchases with original receipts, followed by equipment return to NGC at project completion.

## Capacity Building Components

- Government-department training on freshwater turtle ecology, survey methods, water management and statutory conservation duties.
- Remote training of turtle surveyors in identification, ecology, preferred habitats and survey protocols.
- At least five training sessions for community stakeholders.
- School outreach, community awareness campaigns, annual bird-conservation seminars and village-head meetings.
- IUCN MKKS staff workshops on bird monitoring, data collection and analysis.
- Strengthening of protected-area governance and transfer of monitoring data and guidance to the IUCN MKKS management team.

## Team Requirements

- Firm / JV: REOI requires SECP company registration, Federal Board of Revenue and relevant provincial revenue registration, at least ten years of national conservation-project experience, at least two Biodiversity Management/Action Plans, and protected-area, monitoring and outreach experience. The TOR describes some registration provisions as desirable; the REOI states them as requirements.
- BAP Team Leader / Biodiversity Expert: relevant master’s degree and 15 years of overall experience, including at least two BAP/BMP assignments and long-term team leadership.
- Bird Monitoring Lead / Avifauna Expert: relevant master’s degree and ten years of avifauna experience, monitoring/research leadership, survey methods, analysis and publications.
- River Turtle Expert: relevant master’s degree focused on reptile conservation, ten years of experience, at least two turtle or reptile/amphibian conservation projects, survey training and breeding-programme expertise.
- Forester: relevant master’s degree and ten years of experience, including at least two forest-enhancement projects and native planting/maintenance expertise.
- Community Engagement Coordinator: bachelor’s degree in social sciences and at least three years of community/social mobilization experience.
- Support personnel include an administrator where needed, a four-person bird research team and two trained turtle surveyors. Survey-team qualifications are assigned by the relevant technical lead.
- JV, subconsultancy and association arrangements are permitted. A key expert may not participate in more than two proposals.
